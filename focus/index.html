      THE IDEAS BEHIND THE PRACTICE

      Rooted in something.

      Books offer approaches. Studies offer evidence. Here’s what each contributes.

      ↳

      Tend combines a focus timer with original research prompts. The specific interface, weekly result, and three-question reflection are our adaptations; the sources below have not validated this app.

      01BOOK · 2016

      Deep Work

      Cal Newport · Grand Central Publishing

      Make deliberate room for demanding work, with routines that protect your attention.

      In Tend One question before the timer, a useful stopping point, and Quiet view. These prompts are our adaptation.

      Book & publisher’s introduction ↗
      02BOOK · 2024

      Slow Productivity

      Cal Newport · Portfolio

      Reduce simultaneous commitments, allow a sustainable pace, and give worthwhile work sustained care.

      In Tend One intended weekly result instead of a growing list of competing projects. This is our adaptation of Newport’s approach.

      The author on the book’s principles ↗
      03METHOD

      The Pomodoro Technique

      Francesco Cirillo

      Choose one activity, use work intervals and breaks, and observe interruptions and actual effort.

      In Tend A 25/5 starting rhythm and a longer break after four full sessions. Custom rhythms are available. The method is broader than a timer; 25/5 is not established here as universally optimal.

      The creator’s explanation ↗
      04RESEARCH · META-ANALYSIS · 2006

      Make an if–then plan

      Peter M. Gollwitzer & Paschal Sheeran

      The authors’ meta-analysis found that specifying when and how to act can support goal attainment across studied settings.

      In Tend An optional plan: if you drift, jot down the thought and return. This particular distraction prompt was not tested in the cited analysis.

      Implementation Intentions and Goal Achievement ↗Author-hosted paper (PDF) ↗
      05RESEARCH · SYSTEMATIC REVIEW & META-ANALYSIS · 2022

      Breaks are part of the work

      Patricia Albulescu, Irina Macsinga, Andrei Rusu, Coralia Sulea, Alexandra Bodnaru & Bogdan Tudor Tulbure · PLOS ONE 17(8), e0272460

      Across 22 study samples, short breaks had small beneficial effects on energy and fatigue. The overall performance effect was not statistically significant; demanding work may require longer recovery.

      In Tend A reminder to step away and adjust your break to your needs. This study does not establish the app’s presets as optimal or promise better research performance.
